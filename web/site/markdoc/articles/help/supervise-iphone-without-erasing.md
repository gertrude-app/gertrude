## What is Supervised Mode?

**Supervised Mode** is a special mode initially created by Apple to give schools and
businesses much more control over their iPhones and iPads. But it can also be enabled by
anyone, making it a super useful tool for anyone who wants to more _tightly control or
restrict their iPhone or iPad._

Until recently, supervising your own device required that you own a Mac computer, that you
factory reset and erase the device, and that you use a complicated app called "Apple
Configurator" designed for schools and businesses and not normal people. However, in
January of 2026 Gertrude released a tool that lets you supervise your own device with any
computer, Mac or PC (even one you borrow from a friend) and it does it in about 5 minutes
without erasing your device or losing any data.

## When supervision is necessary

Supervised Mode is generally useful for many things, but maybe the most important is that
it unlocks the ability for adults to **filter and block the internet** across their entire
device.

Apple allows apps like Gertrude to filter and restrict certain parts of the internet,
within _any app_ on the device, including Apple's own apps. This allows Gertrude to remove
unwanted content that otherwise is completely uncontrollable. A good example is the
[**#images** animated GIF search](/help/iphone-ipad/block-gif-search-in-messages) embedded
in Apple's texting app Messages. This feature allows access to hundreds of sexually
explicit and inappropriate images. Many parents don't want their kids to have access to
these images, and many adults don't want this temptation on their devices either.

{% image src="supervise/gif-search-blocked.png" width=600 caption="Gertrude can block GIFs and more for adults in supervised mode" alt="Gertrude blocking explicit results in the #images animated GIF search in the iPhone Messages app" /%}

For children under 18 in an Apple Family, an app like Gertrude can request permission to
filter the internet from the Family organizer. But Apple for some reason doesn't let
normal devices owned by adults over 18 install internet filters. However, **supervised
devices** can install these internet filters.

{% callout type="note" title="Is there an easier way?" %}

If the device can be signed in to a different Apple Account belonging to someone under 18,
that is the easiest path, and
[Apple does permit changing the birthdate](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102473) on an
Apple Account.

{% /callout %}

## What else Supervised Mode unlocks

Here are just a few more things Supervised Mode unlocks:

- internet filtering for adults (see above)
- restrict which apps are allowed and can be installed for adults
- disable Safari, Apple Music, Radio, News, iTunes, Book store, and more
- enforce a VPN, making it impossible to remove
- prevent removal of restriction profiles and other workarounds

## Frequently asked questions

#### Do I lose my data when enabling Supervised Mode?

No. Gertrude's supervision tool supervises the device in place over USB, so there's **no
hard reset and no data loss.** Your apps, photos, messages, and settings are all exactly
where you left them. Apple Configurator, the older method, _does_ erase the device, which
is the main reason we built our own tool.

#### Will I need to reinstall my apps?

No. Because nothing is erased, your apps stay installed and signed in. There's no backup
to restore and no setting the device up again from scratch.

#### Do I need a Mac?

No. The Gertrude supervision tool runs on **Mac and Windows.** That's the biggest
practical difference from Apple Configurator, which is Mac-only. On a Windows PC you'll
need iTunes installed so Windows can talk to the device. You can also borrow a computer,
since it only takes a few minutes and isn't needed again afterward.

#### What's the difference between Screen Time and Supervised Mode?

Screen Time is Apple's built-in parental controls, and works on any iPhone or iPad.
Supervised Mode is a deeper, device-level setting originally built for schools and
businesses, and it unlocks restrictions Screen Time can't reach, including letting an
adult install an internet content filter on their own device. They're not alternatives,
and most people who supervise a device use both together.

#### Can I supervise an iPhone I already own and use?

Yes, and that's exactly what this is for. Supervision used to be something you could only
turn on when a device was first set up, which is why Apple's own documentation says an
administrator has to completely erase the device. Gertrude supervises a phone you're
already using, with everything on it left intact.

#### Why do I have to turn off Find My iPhone?

Find My enables **Activation Lock,** which blocks the kind of device-level change that
supervision makes. Turning it off temporarily lets supervision go through, and you can
turn Find My back on as soon as it's finished.

#### How do I remove supervision later?

Supervision is a device-level setting, so it isn't undone by deleting the Gertrude app or
cancelling your subscription. [Contact Gertrude support](/contact) and we'll send you an
unsupervision tool once we've double-checked your identity.

## What about Apple Configurator?

**Apple Configurator** is Apple's own tool for putting a device into Supervised Mode, and
before our tool existed it was the only real option. It still works, but it comes with two
significant costs: it runs on a **Mac** only, requires frequent access to the Mac for
ongoing management, and it **erases the device** completely. You'd need a current backup,
and app data in particular often doesn't survive the round trip.

Avoiding both of those is exactly why we built our own
[supervision tool for iPhone and iPad](/iphone-and-ipad). But if you'd rather use Apple's,
or you're setting up a device you were going to wipe anyway, we've kept
[step-by-step Apple Configurator instructions](/help/iphone-ipad/supervise-iphone-with-apple-configurator).
