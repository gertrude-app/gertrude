The
[Gertrude Blocker iOS app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gertrude-blocker/id6736368820)
was initially created to plug a glaring gap in Apple's Screen Time parental controls,
introduced in iOS 17, when parents lost the ability to
[block `#images` GIF search in Messages](/help/iphone-ipad/block-gif-search-in-messages).
To this day, it is one of the only apps that allows parents and accountability partners to
block these GIF searches and images.

One of the most common questions we get is whether Gertrude can also block GIF search and
display on an Apple Watch. Sadly, the answer is: **only partially.** Apple does not let us
install our internet filter on the watch itself. However, when a watch is connected to its
companion iPhone [running Gertrude](/iphone-and-ipad), the blocking does work. But when
the watch is using its own Wi-Fi or cellular connection, the GIF search and display are
available again.

## Can you block `#images` GIFs on an Apple Watch?

- The official tool Apple gives us that we use to block GIFs on iPhone and iPad, called a
  ["content filter"](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/networkextension/nefilterdataprovider),
  is officially unsupported and unavailable on watchOS, including the most recent
  versions.
- Apple currently does not provide a setting in Screen Time or anywhere else that disables
  `#images` GIF search in Messages on Apple Watch.
- Multiple parents have reported to us that an Apple Watch connected to its companion
  iPhone running Gertrude does indeed block GIFs. We believe this is due to the watch
  using the iPhone's internet connection (complete with filtering).
- Parents have also reported that when the watch is away from the iPhone (or if it is a
  standalone Apple Watch set up without a companion iPhone), there is no blocking of GIFs.
- On both iPhones and Apple Watches, previously downloaded and viewed GIFs from any
  session remain visible and accessible for a long time, as they are stored in the
  device's cache. So even if a watch comes back into the range of a Gertrude-protected
  iPhone, already cached GIF images remain visible.

## The Apple Watch parental-control trap

Parents often think that getting their child just an Apple Watch is a way to allow basic
communication without the risks of a full smartphone. While this is a reasonable
assumption, the lack of ability to block GIFs makes an Apple Watch considerably less safe
than many parents expect. Many GIFs available in the `#images` iMessage app when texting
are sexually suggestive or explicit and have been widely known to be a gateway towards
pornography addictions for many young people.

Apple explicitly markets this setup to families under the name
[**Apple Watch For Your Kids (formerly Family Setup)**](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109036).
But we would fairly strongly advise against this strategy and would recommend just waiting
longer to get your child a properly protected iPhone several years later. None of my kids
have had an Apple Watch, and none has had an iPhone until near their 16th birthday, and
when they received one, it was
[heavily restricted with Gertrude and Screen Time](/guides/locking-down-an-iphone).
