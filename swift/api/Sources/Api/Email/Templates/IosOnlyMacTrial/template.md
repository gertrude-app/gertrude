Now more backstory... Gertrude actually originally started as ONLY a Mac app, and it's the
oldest and still the most powerful app in our lineup. It's the app I wished existed a few
years ago when my kids started doing more and more of their homework online. I built it
because I was frustrated with all of the existing apps for two basic reasons: 1) they
weren't strict enough, and 2) they were too hard to use.  So I wrote the Gertrude Mac app
to fix those two things. Originally, it was just an experiment for my two oldest kids.
Then my brother's family started using it too. Then families from my church started asking
for it as well, and it slowly grew from there.

Gertrude for Mac is awesome for a couple reasons. First, it's the safest internet filter
out there, because it's the only one designed to start by BLOCKING EVERYTHING, forcing the
parent to say what is allowed. Most filters work the other way, trying to categorize and
block the bad stuff. But there's just too much bad stuff, and too many new websites being
created every day for this to work. So Gertrude flips that around and says every website
is guilty until proven innocent. Parents get to "unlock" parts they know are safe and
useful -- school websites, online classes, church... stuff like that.

Second, it adds a layer of accountability by constantly taking SCREENSHOTS of what your
kids are doing on their computer. Parents get to review these on their own time. What's
critical is that we don't hide this -- it's not spying. Gertrude on Mac loudly advertises
that the screen is being recorded, which creates an incredible transparency dynamic. Kids
(or adults - lots of people now use it with their spouses or accountability partners) know
that their computer activity is not secret, so they self-regulate automatically -- the
same way you would imagine a person would if they were using their computer in a public
place where their screen could be observed. Lots of bad things thrive in the dark, and
Gertrude screenshots turn on the light.
